## What Is ResearchForge?

ResearchForge is an open-source research operating system. The goal is to bring together tools that researchers currently use in isolation — literature search, reference management, note-taking, summarization, and collaboration — into a single, coherent, AI-assisted platform.

The project is not trying to replace Google Scholar, Zotero, or Notion. It is trying to be the layer that connects them — and eventually, goes beyond them.

---

## The Problem We Are Solving

Modern scientific research is information-intensive and tool-fragmented.

A typical researcher today:

- Searches for papers across multiple platforms (Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, arXiv, PubMed)
- Manages references in tools like Zotero or Mendeley
- Takes notes in Notion, Obsidian, or paper notebooks
- Uses ChatGPT or similar tools for summarization — with no connection to their reference library
- Collaborates via email threads, shared Google Docs, or ad hoc Slack messages
- Maintains no structured record of the connections between ideas across papers

The result is that enormous amounts of time are spent on the process of research rather than on research itself. Knowledge gets siloed inside individual researchers rather than building into a shared, connected body of understanding.

ResearchForge aims to solve this by providing:

1. **A unified search layer** that retrieves papers by meaning, not just keyword
2. **A structured knowledge layer** that maps relationships between concepts and papers
3. **An AI assistance layer** that helps researchers understand, summarize, and connect material
4. **A collaboration layer** that enables teams to build shared knowledge together

## Target Users

ResearchForge is being built for:

- **Academic researchers** — PhD students, postdocs, and faculty conducting literature-heavy research
- **Research teams** — Labs, departments, or interdisciplinary teams that need to share a body of knowledge
- **Students** — Undergraduates and postgraduates doing systematic literature reviews or research projects
- **Knowledge workers** — Anyone who regularly reads, synthesizes, and connects information from multiple sources

The project is open-source. We also explicitly welcome contributors from all backgrounds — you don't need to be a researcher to build for researchers.

## Our Long-Term Vision

ResearchForge aspires to be the operating system layer for the entire research lifecycle:

```
Discovery → Organization → Understanding → Synthesis → Collaboration → Publication
```

At each stage, ResearchForge should reduce friction, surface connections, and help the researcher focus on thinking rather than on tooling.

Long-term, we envision a platform where:

- A researcher can describe a topic in natural language and receive a structured map of the existing literature
- Teams can collaboratively annotate and build shared knowledge graphs over time
- AI assistance is context-aware — knowing what the researcher has already read and what connections they are trying to draw
- The platform is extensible — teams can bring their own data sources, models, and workflows

We are at the very beginning of this journey. The vision is ambitious by design — it is meant to guide long-term decisions, not to describe what we will build this year.

## IEEE Summer of Code 2026

ResearchForge is a project under **IEEE Summer of Code 2026**. This means:

- The project has a defined development timeline tied to the IEEE SoC programme
- It is expected to move from planning through to an early implementation milestone during the programme period
- Contributors working under the IEEE SoC programme have defined deliverables and timelines
- The project is also open to external contributors at all times

The IEEE SoC programme encourages projects that combine meaningful technical work with open-source community building. ResearchForge is designed to be both technically ambitious and genuinely accessible to new contributors.

## What Success Looks Like

At the end of the IEEE Summer of Code 2026 programme, success for ResearchForge means:

**For the project:**
- A well-documented, clearly architected codebase with working core functionality
- An active contributor community that continues beyond the programme period
- A clear roadmap to a public beta release

**For contributors:**
- Meaningful, portfolio-worthy contributions to a real open-source project
- Experience with professional open-source workflows (issues, PRs, reviews, conventions)
- A project they can point to as something they helped build

**For researchers (eventually):**
- A tool that genuinely reduces the friction in their work
- A platform they trust enough to use for real research
